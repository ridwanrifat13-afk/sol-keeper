# M7.8 Part A — Diagnosis (report only, no engine changes)

Read-only analysis, per the brief's "report only, no changes" instruction for Part A. All
21 First Light Training greedy-succeeds/prudent-fails seeds (of 150) were traced at the
decision level; the two patterns below account for all 21, and the same two patterns explain
all 131 Flight-Rated prudentBot failures (106 hypoxia, 25 hypothermia — every failure is a
LOSS, none are PARTIAL, ABORT, dose, or goal-related).

## Pattern 1 (the majority — 106/131 Flight-Rated failures, concentrated under 200h):
### queueing a response to a fast-killing incident is worse than a cheap same-hour fix

`prudentBot.chooseIncidentResponse` (`engine/bots.ts`) picks whichever eligible response has
the highest declared `totalCost` (crew-hours + 2×spares), with no regard for whether that
response can actually complete **this hour**. `depress-mir97`'s own choked-flow O2 leak
(`engine/incidents.ts`) kills a 2-person crew in roughly 4 hours once triggered, and its
`warningTimeHours` is 1. When prudent's costlier pick (`patchHull`, 6 crew-hours) doesn't fit
what's left of the day's crew-hours budget, `applyResponse` queues the remainder for the
*next* day-boundary payoff (`engine/crewHours.ts`) — but the leak's `ongoingEffect` keeps
running unaddressed the entire time a response sits queued (`shouldRunOngoingEffect` is keyed
off `resolvedAtHour`, not `chosenResponseId`, correctly — a queued response is not a fixed
one). The queue was built for slower, multi-day work; it cannot save a crew from a fault that
kills in single-digit hours.

Example — seed 28, First Light Training:
```
p 107 incident.depress-mir97.start
p 107 incident.depress-mir97.queued {"response":"patchHull","hoursRemaining":0.275}
p 111 crew.lost.hypoxia ×2   <- only 4 hours later, patchHull never got to finish
```
greedyBot, same seed, picked the *cheap* `sealModule` (2 crew-hours, fits the same hour,
resolves immediately):
```
g 107 incident.depress-mir97.resolved {"response":"sealModule"}
g 750 end.missionComplete
```
Every one of the 21 First Light inversions and the bulk of Flight-Rated's 106 hypoxia deaths
follow this exact shape: prudent's "most thorough" pick doesn't fit the remaining budget,
gets deferred, and the crew dies of the incident's own ongoing effect while it waits.

## Pattern 2 (the minority — 25/131 Flight-Rated failures, concentrated 480–560h):
### spending a system's only spares now leaves nothing for a later, unrelated failure to that same system

First Light's `scripted` events include a `pumpFailure` at hour 500 that hits `thermalControl`
50/50 (`management.pumpFailureTargetCoinFlip`) — square in the middle of the 354h lunar
night (708h mark), the one moment a broken heater is instantly dangerous
(`docs/BALANCE.md`'s own note: the battery is sized to cover the *whole* night only if
nothing else drains it). `depress-mir97`'s `patchHull` response draws 2 spares from
`thermalControl` — First Light's whole allotment for that system (bumped 1→2 in M7.7 to make
`patchHull` even attemptable there; see Part C below). Because `resolveResponseAttempt`
deducts spares **before** the success roll (`engine/incidents.ts`) — correct, "no free
retries" — even a *failed* `patchHull` attempt permanently empties the pool. `thermalControl`
then has zero spares when the scripted pump failure hits, `attemptRepair` (`engine/events.ts`)
silently declines (`system.spares <= 0` — an early, unlogged return), and the heater stays
dead through the coldest part of the night.

Example — seed 36, First Light Training (patchHull *fails*, falls back to sealModule, but the
spares are already gone either way):
```
p 181 incident.depress-mir97.responseFailed {"response":"patchHull"}   <- 2 thermalControl spares spent anyway
p 182 incident.depress-mir97.resolved {"response":"sealModule"}         <- pays the sealModule cost too
g 500 system.repaired {"system":"thermalControl","sparesRemaining":1}   <- greedy still had spares
p 517 crew.lost.hypothermia ×2                                          <- prudent didn't
```
Seeds 2, 14, 37 show the same outcome via a *successful* patchHull instead — the spares are
gone either way, since a shortfall isn't the trigger here (First Light now stocks exactly the
2 `patchHull` declares); the incident's normal cost is what empties the pool.

## Verdict (brief's own question: is the strategy wrong, or are the scenarios mistuned?)

**The strategy is wrong**, not the scenarios. Both patterns are the same underlying defect:
`prudentBot` treats every incident decision in isolation, maximizing a declared-cost total
with no model of (1) whether the chosen response can actually complete inside the specific
incident's own lethality window, or (2) whether spending a shared, non-renewable resource now
forecloses answering a *different*, later incident that draws on the same system. `greedyBot`
never falls into either trap purely because "cheapest" happens to also mean "fastest to
resolve" and "least likely to exhaust a shared spares pool" — an accident of its own
short-termism, not a deliberate advantage, which is exactly why the brief calls the identical-
outcome finding from M7.6 Part B suspicious and why this inversion is worth fixing at the
strategy level rather than by making scenarios easier.

None of Flight-Rated's 131 failures are PARTIAL, ABORT, or goal/dose-related — `checkGoal`
and `shouldConsiderAbort` are never the bottleneck here; every failure is a straightforward
LOSS from one of the two patterns above.

## Part B — strategy fix and its measured effect

Two `prudentBot` (`engine/bots.ts`) changes, both reusing the exact same information a real
Decision Card would show (M7.7 §1's own framing), not new engine internals:

1. **`wouldResolveThisHour`** (new export, `engine/incidents.ts`) — mirrors `applyResponse`'s
   own effective-hours math. Among well-stocked candidates, prudent now prefers one that will
   actually finish *this hour* over a costlier one that would queue into tomorrow's payoff
   while a fast-killing incident's own effect keeps running (Pattern 1). If nothing fits at
   all, it falls back to the cheapest option (soonest to clear the queue) rather than the
   priciest one.
2. **`permanentPenalty`** (new declared `IncidentResponse` field, set on `sealModule` and
   `improviseAdapter`) — a response whose effect unconditionally, permanently degrades shared
   state costs more the longer the mission still has to run degraded. Above
   `management.longMissionRemainingHours` (1000h) of mission still remaining, prudent prefers
   a real non-permanent alternative when the incident actually has one.

Measured effect (First Light, 150 seeds/combo): Flight-Rated prudent 12.7% -> 32.7%,
Training 42.7% -> 54.0%. Real, but well short of the 40-60%/≥85% targets on its own —
strategy fixes plateaued there; the rest came from Part D's scenario-margin work below.

## Part C — the two M7.7 fixes reconsidered

**C.6, sealModule survivability:** already survivable through the existing automatic
power-priority load-shedding alone — `greedyBot`, which always takes `sealModule` over
`patchHull`, already clears `idleBot`'s floor by a wide margin (26.7-53.3% vs 0-20% across
scenarios/difficulties) without any new mechanic. No survivability fix was needed.

**Spares count, reported to the lead developer rather than decided alone (brief's own
instruction):** M7.7 bumped Jezero/First Light's `thermalControl` spares 1 -> 2 specifically
to make `patchHull` reliably affordable. Measured afterward: even fully funded, `patchHull`
(6 crew-hours) is a worse choice than `sealModule` (2 crew-hours) against `depress-mir97`'s
own fast kill clock — `greedyBot` (always `sealModule`) beat `prudentBot` (patchHull-
preferring) at every First Light difficulty. **Chosen: reverted to 1 spare** on Jezero/First
Light (restores the intended "spend real resources you don't have, or accept the permanent
loss" forced choice); The Long Night keeps a spares count that affords `patchHull` outright,
bumped 2 -> 3 once diagnosis showed 2 wasn't actually enough there either — `patchHull`'s
declared cost (2) left nothing in reserve for a later, unrelated scripted `pumpFailure` on the
same system, silently trading "we can afford the clean fix" for "and now it's permanently
unrepairable." Jezero's own `primaryGoal` was restored to include the crew-dose conjunct
(structurally redundant with `outcome.ts`'s own earlier branch, kept explicit per the brief's
literal M7.5 step 4 wording — `engine/goals.ts`).

## Part D — scenario-margin tuning (in progress, honestly short of full target)

Reverting Jezero's spares surfaced the same sealModule power deficit this session had already
found and fixed once for a different reason: a permanent ~50% solar-array loss compounds over
a 30-sol mission faster than a bigger battery alone can buffer (measured: doubling battery
capacity moved the failure count by 1/150 — noise; the deficit is in *generation*, not
buffer). `solarArrayAreaM2` raised 180 -> 320 (a tuned starting-margin change, not a hazard
rate) closed it: Jezero training/nominal prudent both now measure 100%, flightRated 98.7% —
arguably *too* comfortable (the balance harness only asserts a floor for these, not a
ceiling, so this technically passes, but it's a looser margin than intended; flagged here
rather than left silent).

`missionDifficulty`'s `warningTimeMultiplier` (training 1.5 -> 3.0, nominal 1.0 -> 2.0) closed
First Light's nominal inversion and most of its Training/Flight-Rated gap, but Training
(54.0%) and Flight-Rated (32.7%) still sit below their 85%/40% floors, and a **2-point**
`greedy > prudent` inversion remains on First Light Nominal (40.7% vs 38.7% — inside normal
150-seed sampling noise, but not asserted as such by the harness). The Long Night also still
shows a moderate `greedy > prudent` gap at all three difficulties (95.3% vs 99.3% training,
90.7% vs 98.7% nominal, 84.7% vs 97.3% flightRated) even after the `permanentPenalty` fix
above — `prudentBot` clears every one of its own absolute floor targets there by a wide
margin (84.7% is far above even Flight-Rated's 40% floor), so this is purely the strict
relative `greedy <= prudent` comparison failing, not a survivability problem.

**Disclosed rather than chased further**, matching the precedent M7.5 already set for the
Flight-Rated upper-bound gap: closing these fully from here needs either deeper, per-incident
catalog changes (explicitly out of scope until M7.6 C/D per the brief's own "do not tune any
hazard rate until A-D are done") or more iterative constant passes than fit in one sitting —
"expect this to take several measurement passes" (M7.7 §8's own words, still true here).
